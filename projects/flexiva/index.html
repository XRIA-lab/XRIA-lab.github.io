FLEXIVA

Understanding and Overcoming the Barriers to Industrial Energy Flexibility

Duration: 2027–2031
Programme: RESILIENT Competence Centre for Resilient Energy Systems
Project budget: SEK 2 million
Partners: Scania, the USER – Uppsala Smart Energy Research group, and Uppsala University (XRIA Lab)
Status: Upcoming

As the energy system shifts toward variable renewable generation, industry can contribute to a more robust grid by adapting when and how it uses electricity. In practice, however, this industrial energy flexibility is rarely realized: it is unclear where the flexibility lies, what it takes to provide it without disrupting production, and whether it pays off. FLEXIVA addresses these barriers together with Scania, in three connected steps.

1. Screen: where is the flexibility potential? Analyze load profiles and production patterns across equipment and processes, using energy management system (EMS) and production data, to identify and prioritize potential flexibility assets.

2. Validate: what does it take to provide flexibility? Study selected energy-intensive processes in depth, covering operational characteristics, production constraints, and equipment risks, to determine the technically and operationally feasible flexibility potential.

3. Valuate: what is the economic case? Assess costs and benefits under different implicit and explicit flexibility models and incentive structures, to determine the economic viability of providing flexibility.

The project builds on XRIA Lab’s work on simulation, simulation-based optimization, and energy efficiency in production systems, and on USER’s expertise in flexible electricity use and smart grids. It is part of the extension of RESILIENT, a competence centre working to make the Swedish energy system more reliable and resource-efficient with low environmental impact.
