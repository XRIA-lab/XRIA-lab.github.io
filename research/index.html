Research

Industry is moving toward human-centered, resilient, and sustainable production (Industry 5.0). XRIA Lab studies how extended reality and artificial intelligence can put people at the center of that transition: helping operators acquire skills, giving managers explainable decision support, and making production systems more energy-efficient. Our work is organized around three connected themes.

XR & Human Augmentation

We develop XR systems that connect operators to live machine data, deliver adaptive step-by-step guidance, and capture expert knowledge so it can be transferred to new staff. Current work combines XR headsets with multimodal sensing, computer vision, and AI agents that assess workload, posture, and task progress.

Projects: MANUFACTOR, ARTISAN, VFS2024

Publications in this theme

AI Decision Support & Simulation-Based Optimization

We use discrete-event simulation, simulation-based optimization with knowledge discovery, reinforcement learning, and large language models to support production planning and energy efficiency. Examples include aligning production schedules with renewable energy availability, identifying and valuing industrial energy flexibility, and generating and adapting simulation models automatically with generative AI.

Projects: AI-COMPETE, FLEXIVA (from 2027), MANUFACTOR

Publications in this theme

XR in Engineering Education

We design and evaluate XR virtual labs for engineering courses, such as HoloMech for solid mechanics and an XR environment for machine design, and study their effect on student motivation, spatial reasoning, and cognitive load.

Projects: PUMA2023 – HoloMech, PUMA2025 · see also our courses

Publications in this theme

Facilities

Our work is developed and tested with XR headsets, including Microsoft HoloLens, and in a learning factory environment where XR applications interact with real production equipment and live machine data.
